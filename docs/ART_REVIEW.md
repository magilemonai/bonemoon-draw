# Art review: the first 94

A read of every painting delivered on 2026-09-06, laid out on contact sheets and checked against the brief. Verdict first, then the standouts, then the touch-ups, then what the interface now does with the art.

## Verdict

The set reads as one deck. Ninety-four images from one brief, and you could shuffle them together and nobody would find the seams: the same indigo night, the same gouache-and-ink surface, the same warm lantern light, the tarot habit of one centered subject with its symbols placed on purpose. That consistency is the hardest thing to get from an image model and the most valuable thing here. The report was honest about the five that still nudge the safe area, and none of those five is a problem on the table.

The paintings are now the best-looking thing in the game, and the interface has been rebuilt to show them large (see the last section).

## Standouts

Cards that are exactly what the world needed:

- **The Magician (Luigi Castanata).** One hand up, one down, the infinity sign, and the four suit objects laid on the library table: a sun medallion, antlers, a cup with a wave, a gear. That is the Rider-Waite Magician translated into Valisar without a word of explanation.
- **The High Priestess (Nitriti).** The mist body, the porcelain masks with too many eyes and no mouths, the pillars, the crescent, the scroll, the lantern. Every element from the campaign notes and every element from the traditional card, in one image.
- **The Emperor.** The throne is empty and radiant, and seven crowned silhouettes stand behind it. That is the false dynasty in a single picture. Better than a portrait would have been.
- **Portraits.** All six carry at 48 pixels. Luigi with Brog on his shoulder and blue flame in his fingers is the best of them. Shazz pouring tea with the spider in the window and a chained goat-devil on the shelf behind him is a card you can read twice.
- **The Hermit.** Luigi on the mountain with the lantern, and Brog as a small dark blob with two eyes at his feet. Quietly perfect.
- **The Moon.** Bone moon, two towers, a wolf and a dog, dead trees. The traditional card, made Valisar by the moon being too big.
- **The Tower (The Fallen Isle).** The island coming down with its chains snapping, the sunset behind it. The best pure image in the set.
- **Fin & Bin.** Two twig children in the crook of an oak. Adorable and correct.
- **The Garbage Boyz.** T.H.U.N.K. on treads with the dwarf and the tiny druid with the oar riding on top, nine cups stacked in the forklift. Someone read the notes.
- **Bimp Bossington.** Three raccoons in a trenchcoat holding a tea tray with four cups on it. And the Raccoon token, holding a tiny tea tray, looking guilty.
- **Mr. Zero.** A gentleman of mirror panes in a top hat, nine gears at his feet. **Mordeaux** with the cuckoo. **Mr. Boscoe** with his head floating free of his body. The Gears court is the funniest suit and it stays in style.
- **Kojin.** The whip of fire over the forge with seven suns set into the stone. **The Heartwood.** **Justice** (the silver stag with the scales in its antlers). **Serratta** as the kraken king under the sunken city. **The card back.**

## Where it is weaker

None of these needs fixing before anyone plays. They are the notes for a second pass.

1. **Pip objects sometimes sit on the scene instead of in it.** The Tides cups are the model: in the boat, on the rock, stacked on the forklift, arranged on the sea. The antlers in Antlers 3, 6, 7, and 9 lie on the ground like bones and read as clutter; the gears in Gears 7 and 10 are a row of props on the floor; the five suns around Vraxxis float as stars. A regeneration note for those seven: work the objects into architecture or into the subject (antlers on a shrine, gears in the machine, suns as medallions on the ash spirit's chest).
2. **A few lore details drifted.** Cernis (King of Antlers) is an antlered hooded figure; the notes give him the face of a hare, which is stranger and better. Vel (Queen of Suns) is a calm red-haired woman with a sun disc; the notes want skin that shimmers like heat haze, and a hint of the countdown. Brog (token) is a dark crystal ball beside a lamp; he should be a small fuzzy blob. Esmerelda Gotch's chains of keys and teeth are barely there.
3. **Everything is night.** That is the brief and it is right for the deck, but on a board where two suits sit side by side, Antlers and Tides are close in value. The suit ink in the card frame does the separating. If a second pass ever happens, one dusk or dawn image per suit would give the Codex some breathing room.
4. **Tall subjects meet a wide window.** Card faces show the painting cropped to 5:4, so the Hanged Man's rope, the top of the Heartwood, the Tidecaller's pommel, and the helmet on the Aurium Plate get trimmed on the card. The crop now favors the top of the painting, and the inspect view shows the whole square. Nothing to regenerate.
5. **The Hierophant has 31 singers.** The report says so itself. Nobody will count.

## Touch-up list for a second pass (optional)

In priority order, with the fix in one line each:

- `suns-5` Vraxxis: the five suns as medallions set into the ash-and-ember body, in place of the floating stars.
- `antlers-king` Cernis: give him the hare's face under the antlers.
- `tok-brog` Brog: a small round fuzzy blue-black blob with two eyes, on the table next to the lamp.
- `suns-queen` Vel: heat-haze shimmer on the skin, faint arc-light under it; keep the pose.
- `antlers-3`, `antlers-6`, `antlers-7`, `antlers-9`: antlers mounted (a shrine, a lintel, a cairn, a saddle) instead of lying loose.
- `gears-7`, `gears-10`: gears built into the vault floor pattern or the machine, so they stop reading as props.
- `gears-queen` Gotch: more chains, keys, and teeth hanging from the shawl; the gear can go.

## What the interface now does with the art

- **Inspect** is built around the painting: a 420-pixel hero with a toggle that turns it Reversed (the picture rotates and takes the bruise wash) beside the two faces as parchment strips and the flavor line. Tap any card, anywhere, to get there.
- **The Codex** opens in a Paintings view: square tiles, four across on a laptop, two on a phone. A Cards view is one tap away.
- **The face chooser** shows the painting beside the two faces when you pick a card from your hand.
- **Choose your Significator** uses the portraits.
- **Card faces** crop the painting toward its upper half so heads and emblems survive the 5:4 window.
